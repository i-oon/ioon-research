## Foundations: World Models, JEPA, and Action-Relevant Latent Spaces

### Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures as World Models

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) are a class of self-supervised models that learn by predicting the embedding of one signal from another, instead of reconstructing raw observations, which makes them particularly well-suited for world modeling and action-relevant representation learning.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) In a canonical JEPA formulation, the model consists of a context encoder \(f_\theta\), a target encoder \(f_{\bar{\theta}}\) (often an exponential moving average of the context encoder), and a predictor \(g_\phi\) that maps context embeddings into predicted target embeddings.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) Given a context signal \(x\) (for example, a masked video clip) and a target signal \(y\) (for example, the masked-out future frames), the JEPA is trained to minimize a latent prediction loss of the general form
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,y}\Big[\ell\big(g_\phi(f_\theta(x)), f_{\bar{\theta}}(y)\big)\Big],
\]
where \(\ell\) is typically a squared error or contrastive loss in latent space.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) Because the objective acts on abstract representations instead of pixels, the model can focus on predictable, semantically meaningful structure while discarding high-entropy, task-irrelevant details such as texture noise, making the learned latents more action-relevant and computationally efficient than reconstruction-based alternatives.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)

A JEPA becomes a **world model** when its inputs \(x\) and \(y\) correspond to temporally related observations and its predictor incorporates information about actions or temporal indices, yielding latent dynamics of the form \(z_{t+1} \approx g_\phi(z_t, a_t)\).[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) In this setting, the JEPA learns a predictive latent space in which future states of the environment and agent can be imagined by rolling forward in latent space conditioned on actions, without reconstructing observations at each step.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) Importantly, the anti-collapse mechanisms used in JEPA—such as predictive masking, architectural asymmetries between context and target branches, or auxiliary diversity losses—are critical for avoiding trivial representations where all inputs map to the same embedding.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) These architectural features underpin V-JEPA 2 and its action-conditioned variant, which we will examine in detail later.

World-model-based reinforcement learning algorithms such as DreamerV3 adopt a related but more classical latent dynamics approach, in which a representation model encodes observations into latent states, a transition model predicts future latent states conditioned on actions, and separate actor and critic networks are trained concurrently using the imagined rollouts.[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) DreamerV3’s world model thus implements a latent Markov decision process with dynamics \(z_{t+1} = f_\omega(z_t, a_t)\), reward prediction \(r_t = r_\psi(z_t, a_t)\), and value estimation \(V_\nu(z_t)\), all trained from replayed experience with fixed hyperparameters across domains.[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>) While DreamerV3 emphasizes control performance via policy optimization, JEPA-based world models emphasize representation quality and predictive consistency, but both share the core idea that learning a compact, predictive latent dynamics model can unify perception, prediction, and planning across varied environments.[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)

For the cross-embodiment locomotion problem, the JEPA perspective is particularly attractive because it allows us to enforce that the latent space aligns temporally predictive embeddings of egocentric video without requiring access to explicit actions or rewards during pretraining.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) It also decouples representation learning from downstream control, which is crucial when the target embodiment (the Unitree B1) cannot provide demonstration data or a kinematic model. Latent action models and hierarchical variants of JEPA world models can then be used to extract action-like structures and skills from actionless video, bridging toward the desired morphology-agnostic body-motion coordinate.

### Prediction over Reconstruction and Action Relevance in Video Latents

A central question for any cross-embodiment control framework that learns solely from egocentric video is how to ensure that the learned latent representations are **action-relevant**, meaning that they capture the aspects of the visual dynamics that are caused by and informative for agent motion, rather than static appearance or exogenous scene changes.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) Recent work explicitly studies this question in the context of video world models, arguing that predictive objectives over future representations rather than reconstructive objectives over pixels are the key to making latents useful for control.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) In particular, Yeom et al. compare video world models trained to reconstruct input frames with models trained to predict future latent representations, showing that predictive training yields latents whose features align more strongly with controllable degrees of freedom, enabling better downstream performance in control tasks.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)

The intuition is straightforward. Reconstruction-based objectives force the latent space to carry detailed information about textures, lighting, and other high-entropy aspects of the scene that may be largely independent of the agent’s actions, whereas predictive JEPA-style objectives encourage the model to focus on structures that are both predictable and causally linked to agent motion, such as egocentric camera translation and rotation, moving obstacles, and body-posture changes.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) This distinction is crucial when building morphology-agnostic latent actions from egocentric video, where the only available signal about the agent’s motion is the pattern of image changes over time. A predictive objective can implicitly learn to factor out static background and treat camera motion and body oscillations as the primary drivers of latent transitions, while reconstruction would tend to entangle these with appearance-specific details.

Thus, for the proposed cross-embodiment locomotion framework, we can draw a concrete design principle from this evidence: both the insect and B1 world models should be trained using predictive, JEPA-style latent objectives on masked egocentric video, not autoencoding or frame-reconstruction losses.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) The latent action extraction mechanisms and cross-embodiment alignment losses should then be built on top of these predictive latents, as they are more likely to reflect the underlying SE(2) body-motion dynamics that we wish to capture and transfer.

### Latent Dynamics, Partial Observability, and Belief-Space World Models

World models generally operate under partial observability, especially when inputs are egocentric video without full state information, and the design of the latent dynamics must account for uncertainty about hidden variables such as unobserved obstacles, contact states, or body configuration.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>) Standard JEPA and Dreamer-style world models use vector-valued latent states and deterministic or stochastic predictors, which often implicitly average over possible futures when trained under teacher-forcing, potentially dissipating useful uncertainty about counterfactual outcomes.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) The Unitary World Model JEPA (UWM-JEPA) addresses this by replacing vector latents with density-matrix latents \(\rho\) on a joint system-environment space and using a learned unitary predictor \(U\) such that
\[
\rho_{t+1} = U \rho_t U^\dagger,
\]
preserving the spectrum (eigenvalues) of the joint state exactly during rollout.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>) Because unitary evolution preserves the entropy of the latent state, the predictor cannot dissipate the represented uncertainty, allowing the model to maintain a belief over compatible hidden futures during blind imagination.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>)

In tasks that require forward simulation under counterfactual actions with masked target observations, UWM-JEPA achieves higher accuracy and more robust degradation under action perturbations compared to vector-latent LSTM-JEPA baselines, and under blind rollout it loses far less probe \(R^2\) at short horizons than vector-latent baselines.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>) Critically, UWM-JEPA’s analysis emphasizes that **latent geometry and predictor dynamics matter** for action sensitivity in partially observed environments, not just the context-encoding capacity.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) This has direct implications for egocentric locomotion world models: the camera sees only a limited cone of the environment and the body, and many physically relevant variables (foot contact forces, joint torques) are hidden, so preserving uncertainty in latent dynamics may be essential for robust planning and cross-embodiment transfer.

For the proposed morphology-agnostic body-motion coordinate, we must balance simplicity against this need to represent uncertainty. A full density-matrix latent may be overkill for locomotion control, but the principle that the latent dynamics should not collapse uncertainty prematurely before a planning or control module can exploit it is important.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>) One potential compromise is to use a JEPA world model with stochastic latent transitions and auxiliary contrastive probes that encourage the latents to maintain multiple possible SE(2) body-motion trajectories consistent with the observed egocentric video, particularly when the terrain ahead is partially occluded.

## Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling and the Egocentric–Allocentric Choice

*Merged in from a companion review: this paper (Hu, Chen, and Lipson, arXiv 2207.03386, and its journal extension in* Nature *Communications Engineering, s44182-025-00031-6) was missing from the pass above despite being directly load-bearing for this project's own viewpoint work. Its central finding — a legged robot can learn task-agnostic dynamics purely from a single first-person camera, with no IMU, no kinematic model, and no prior morphology — is the closest published precedent to the egocentric-video-only constraint this project operates under, and the egocentric-versus-allocentric distinction it sits inside is the same axis this project's own Q1/Q2 thread (pose-redundancy under a third-person view, its removal under an egocentric one) has been measuring empirically.*

### Hu, Chen, and Lipson: Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Dynamics Prediction and Adaptation

Hu et al. formulate robot self-modeling as **visual dynamics prediction**: a parametric model \(f_\theta\) maps a short window of past egocentric frames and motor commands, plus a planned future command sequence, to a predicted future state,
\[
\hat{s}_{t+\Delta t} = f_\theta\big(o_{t-k+1:t},\; a_{t-k+1:t+\Delta t}\big),
\]
with \(k=5\) frames of history in their implementation.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Aut...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03386v3>)[Egocentric visual self-modeling for aut...](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-025-00031-6>) Training data comes from **random motor babbling** — no task, no reward — while a separately trained Visual Odometry (VO) model supplies the state-change labels \(s_{t+\Delta t}\) from egocentric video alone, so the whole pipeline is self-supervised end to end: VO turns video into motion labels, and the self-model learns to predict those same labels from video plus action.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Aut...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03386v3>) The training objective is a plain regression loss over predicted-versus-VO-estimated state change,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{dyn}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \left\| \hat{s}_{t_i+\Delta t} - s_{t_i+\Delta t} \right\|_2^2,
\]
with no reconstruction term and no explicit kinematic model anywhere in the loop.[Egocentric visual self-modeling for aut...](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-025-00031-6>) Architecturally this is a CNN frame encoder feeding a temporal aggregator (GRU/LSTM/temporal conv) fused with an encoded action sequence, then an MLP head regressing \(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta\theta\) — considerably lighter than a JEPA or V-JEPA2-scale backbone, and notably a **reconstruction-free, non-JEPA** precedent for the same "predict state, not pixels" principle this project's own encoder-frozen pipeline follows.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Aut...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03386v3>)

Once trained, the model is used as an internal simulator for model-predictive control: candidate action sequences are rolled forward in the learned dynamics and scored against a target state change, entirely without executing them physically first.[Egocentric visual self-modeling for aut...](<https://www.nature.com/articles/s44182-025-00031-6>) Three results are the paper's core contribution. First, a legged robot performs basic locomotion (forward, backward, turning) from egocentric vision alone, with no IMU, no GPS, no prior kinematics — direct evidence that vision-only dynamics prediction is sufficient for locomotion control in principle. Second, models trained purely in simulation transfer to the real robot with no additional physical training. Third, and most distinctive: the self-model doubles as a **damage detector**. Because VO keeps producing correct motion labels regardless of body damage while the self-model's prediction depends on an intact body-to-motion mapping, a sustained rise in prediction error
\[
e_t = \left\| f_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(o_{t-k+1:t}, a_{t-k+1:t+\Delta t}) - s_{t+\Delta t}^{\text{VO}} \right\|_2
\]
signals that the body has changed.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Aut...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03386v3>) The robot then collects new babbling data under the damaged body, with VO relabeling from vision alone, and retrains \(f_\theta\) purely self-supervised — no external diagnosis of what broke, no new sensors, no hand-authored fault model.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Aut...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2207.03386v3>)[Self-supervised robot self-modeling usi...](<https://colab.ws/articles/10.21203%2Frs.3.rs-2303274%2Fv1>) The mechanism generalizes past "damage" in the literal sense: **the same detect-and-retrain loop is exactly what a change of embodiment looks like to a self-model that only ever sees egocentric video and action** — which is the framing this project inherits when treating a swap from insect to B1 as a change the world model must survive rather than a special case to hand-engineer around.

### Egocentric versus Allocentric Vision: the Axis, Not a Detail

The distinction is not merely which camera is used but what each viewpoint makes structurally available and structurally hidden, and it recurs across every paper in this space under a different name each time.

**Egocentric** cameras, rigidly attached to the body, couple vision tightly to proprioception: the frame shows the robot's own limbs against the ground it is walking on, and Hu et al.'s central empirical claim is that this coupling alone — no IMU — carries enough signal to predict body-frame motion.[Egocentric Visual Self-Modeling for Leg...](<https://www.catalyzex.com/paper/egocentric-visual-self-modeling-for-legged>) The cost is that egocentric latents are tied to the body frame and to whatever the camera happens to be looking at; two egocentric views of the same behavior from different mount points or angles are not guaranteed to agree, and a policy trained under one mount can fail under another purely because the *visual* mapping changed, independent of any change in dynamics — the viewpoint-shift problem EgoDemoGen was built to address (below).

**Allocentric** cameras, external and world-fixed, observe the robot in global coordinates. Chen, Kwiatkowski, Vondrick, and Lipson's full-body visual self-modeling work is the clearest allocentric counterpart to Hu et al.: instead of predicting joint angles or egocentric state change, it learns a continuous, differentiable occupancy function \(g_\psi(x, q)\) — does spatial point \(x\) contain the robot at configuration \(q\)? — from an external camera watching the robot move randomly.[Full-Body Visual Self-Modeling of Robot...](<http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06389>) This is memory-efficient, kinematically aware, and — notably — supports the *identical* damage-detection-and-recovery loop Hu et al. demonstrate egocentrically: Chen et al.'s robot also detects, localizes, and recovers from damage by updating its self-model from scratch, purely from external visual observation.[Full-Body Visual Self-Modeling of Robot...](<http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06389>)[A Robot Learns to Imagine Itself](<https://www.me.columbia.edu/news/hod-lipson-robot-self-awareness>) That two independent lines of work reach damage-resilience by the same self-supervised route from *opposite* camera placements is the strongest evidence in this literature that **the recovery mechanism is a property of learned visual self-modeling generally, not of one viewpoint** — while the *representation itself* (body-frame velocity vs. global occupancy) remains inescapably viewpoint-dependent.

This is precisely the tension this project's own Q1/Q2 findings measured directly rather than argued from principle: a third-person (allocentric-style) fixed camera made the pose itself encode the command almost completely (single-frame action R² ≈0.78 on the insect), because gait phase is visible and periodic from outside — the world model had no incentive to read the action at all when the frame already gave it away. Moving the camera onto the body (egocentric) broke that redundancy and was the first manipulation in the whole project to move the GATE C conditioning metric. Hu et al.'s framing supplies the mechanism this result is an instance of: **egocentric vision ties the visual signal to body-frame motion in exactly the coordinate a locomotion controller needs, which is also exactly why it does not hand the controller a free readout of global phase the way a fixed external camera does.** The two literatures were making the same point from different directions — one architectural (self-modeling can be built on either viewpoint, with different tradeoffs), one empirical (this project's own measurement of what one specific viewpoint choice does to action-conditioning) — and the merge is worth stating plainly because neither paper set says it in the other's terms.

**Bridging the two viewpoints** is its own active subfield, relevant wherever this project needs a fixed external camera for calibration or measurement (as it currently does, e.g. for body-motion ground truth) alongside an egocentric model for control. EgoDemoGen tackles egocentric viewpoint shift directly — a robot redeployed under a different camera mount fails because actions computed in the old frame are no longer kinematically appropriate — by geometry-aware trajectory retargeting (EgoTrajTransfer) paired with a conditional video-generation model (EgoViewTransfer) that synthesizes egocentric video under the new viewpoint via a self-supervised double-reprojection objective, needing no real multi-viewpoint data.[EgoDemoGen: Egocentric Demonstration Ge...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.22578>) A complementary manipulation-focused approach trains a multi-camera teacher (both a third-person and an egocentric wrist view) and distills it into a single-camera student via a shared-plus-private representation split, enforced with a representation-permutation loss and a time-contrastive loss, so the student's single egocentric view still carries the viewpoint-invariant part of the teacher's representation.[\[PDF\] Visual-Policy Learning Through Mu...](<https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94d315d22a6e1847cc9a83718c8278486aa4179f>) Neither technique is used by this project today, but both name a concrete, reusable answer to "what if the egocentric mount changes" or "what if we want an allocentric measurement to supervise an egocentric model" — a question this project has so far handled by fixing the camera rig once and holding it constant (`render-lock`), rather than by learning invariance to it.

### What This Changes About the Reading List

Placed next to the rest of this review, Hu et al. sharpens rather than duplicates the JEPA/V-JEPA2 material: it is a second, independent, much lighter-weight existence proof that egocentric-only, action-conditioned, reconstruction-free dynamics prediction is sufficient for legged locomotion control — arrived at without any of V-JEPA2's internet-scale pretraining or masked-prediction machinery. That two very different routes (1M-hour video pretraining with a frozen JEPA backbone, versus a small CNN trained from scratch on a single robot's own babbling) converge on "predict state from egocentric video plus action, skip reconstruction" is a stronger argument for the design principle than either paper alone. Where Hu et al. goes further than anything else in this review is the damage/embodiment-change loop: no other cited system demonstrates *retraining under a changed body from egocentric video alone, self-supervised, no new sensors* — which is the closest existing precedent to what this project's own cross-embodiment step (insect → B1) is asking a world model to survive, even though Hu et al. never crosses embodiments, only damage states of one.

## Cross-Embodiment World Models and Latent Action Spaces

### Latent Action-Conditioned Robot World Models (LAC-WM)

The diversity of robot embodiments and action spaces poses a major challenge for building world models that generalize across different robots and can be reused in new embodiments without retraining from scratch.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) The Latent Action-Conditioned Robot World Model (LAC-WM) addresses this by operating within a learned, unified latent action space shared across diverse embodiments instead of conditioning on explicit, embodiment-specific control signals.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) LAC-WM is trained on videos of humans and multiple robots manipulating objects, learning a shared “action language” that encodes the underlying meaning of actions, such as reaching toward an object or lifting it, in a latent representation that transfers across different bodies.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)

Technically, LAC-WM compares two variants: a latent action-conditioned world model and an explicit action-conditioned world model (EAC-WM) that conditions on explicit motion labels or control signals.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) The explicit model tends to learn disjoint action representations across embodiments, causing downstream performance to degrade as more embodiments are added to pretraining, because the latent space becomes fragmented and embodiment-specific.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) In contrast, the LAC-WM’s unified latent action space allows its downstream performance to scale positively with the number of embodiments used during pretraining, improving task success by up to 46.7% on dexterous manipulation and 11.7% on LIBERO benchmarks and increasing success from 82.4% to 92.0% on pick-and-place tasks.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) The core architectural insight is that aligning actions into a shared latent space, rather than representing them as disjoint control vectors, is critical for efficient cross-embodiment learning.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)

For the locomotion-focused project, LAC-WM provides an essential precedent: it shows that a learned latent action space can capture cross-embodiment semantics of actions and that this space can be discovered from heterogeneous video data without requiring explicit alignment of action channels across robots.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) However, LAC-WM still assumes access to action signals during training, which are encoded into the latent action space, and it does not operate purely on egocentric video without any action labels.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>) Extending its principles to actionless egocentric locomotion data will require integrating latent action models that extract action-like structures from observations alone, as in HiLAM, and then training JEPA world models conditioned on these latent actions rather than on raw control signals.

Architecturally, we can imagine a locomotion analogue of LAC-WM where egocentric video sequences from the insect and B1 robots are first passed through a latent action extractor that infers body-motion commands in an SE(2) coordinate (forward, lateral, yaw), and then a world model learns to predict future latents conditioned on these inferred actions.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The latent action extractor must itself be morphology-agnostic, meaning that the body-motion latents reflect motion in the agent’s body-frame without encoding embodiment-specific joint configurations. In this way, the latent action space becomes a shared “locomotion language” across insects and quadrupeds, analogous to LAC-WM’s shared manipulation language across hands and arms.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)

### Demo-JEPA: Cross-Embodiment Imitation via Latent Goals

While LAC-WM focuses on building a shared action-conditioned world model across embodiments, Demo-JEPA tackles the problem of cross-embodiment imitation from a different angle, emphasizing that demonstrations should be treated as specifications of future goals rather than sequences of actions.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) In many robotic imitation learning setups, demonstrations are viewed as action sequences that must be reproduced, which requires shared action spaces, heuristic retargeting, or large-scale multi-embodiment co-training when demonstrations come from humans or robots with different morphology, kinematics, or action spaces.[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) Demo-JEPA instead views demonstrations as implicit instructions about which future states should be realized, not how the demonstrator executed the trajectory, and builds a JEPA-based world model to translate source visual demonstrations into target-compatible future latent trajectories.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)

The Demo-JEPA framework proceeds in stages. First, an action-conditioned JEPA world model is initialized on target robot trajectories, learning latent dynamics conditioned on robot states and actions.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) Given a source demonstration and the current target observation, a “Dreamer predictor” is trained to translate source motion into target-compatible latent goals, effectively mapping the demonstration intent into the target’s latent space.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>) Once this predictor is frozen, the world-model dynamics predictor is further optimized so that action-conditioned rollouts align with Dreamer-predicted latent goals, allowing the target agent to use these latent trajectories as subgoals and realize them through planning under its own learned forward dynamics.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) Crucially, Demo-JEPA avoids any direct action-level correspondence across embodiments, requiring only visual demonstrations plus the target agent’s own interaction experience, and supports flexible imitation across heterogeneous embodiments.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)

For the insect-to-B1 locomotion transfer, Demo-JEPA’s philosophy is highly relevant. It suggests that we should treat insect locomotion video as specifying desired future states of the agent in a shared body-motion latent space, rather than as a sequence of joint commands that must be reproduced.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) The Unitree B1 should then realize these latent goals through its own control dynamics, which may be learned via RL or model-based methods, without ever attempting to match insect joint movements or leg trajectories. Architecturally, this implies a two-stage process similar to Demo-JEPA: first learn a B1 world model and control interface in its own latent space; then learn a translator from insect egocentric video trajectories into B1-compatible body-motion latent goals, possibly using a predictive JEPA trained across both domains.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) The difference, however, is that in our setting there is no target-domain demonstration data at all, so the translation must rely entirely on a morphology-agnostic representation of locomotion extracted from egocentric video, rather than on a target world model trained from its own experience.

### Calibrated Predictive Safety with Action-Conditioned JEPA

In any real deployment of cross-embodiment locomotion, especially with hardware robots like the Unitree B1, safety constraints and execution-time guarantees are paramount. Vision-language-action policies and learned world models can generalize broadly but rarely offer strong guarantees against collisions or kinematic violations during execution.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) In contrast, classical model-based planners respect geometric and kinematic constraints but tend to generalize poorly when the environment or embodiment changes.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) A recent framework attempts to reconcile these by composing an action-conditioned JEPA world model with deterministic, embodiment-specific safety shields to achieve calibrated predictive safety for heterogeneous robots.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

The proposed pipeline operates in a receding-horizon manner. First, a proposer generates \(K\) candidate action chunks for the current robot embodiment.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) An action-conditioned JEPA world model then rolls each candidate forward in a frozen-encoder latent space that is conditioned on an embodiment embedding, predicting both task progress and physical risk for each rollout.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) Calibrated risk and progress heads score each rollout and report uncertainty, allowing the system to rank candidates in terms of expected success and safety.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) A deterministic safety shield, specific to the embodiment, filters out inadmissible candidates based on geometric and kinematic constraints, and a fallback ladder handles cases where no candidates remain admissible.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) The key architectural claim is a strict separation of responsibilities: the JEPA world model provides a calibrated ranking over candidate actions, but all safety guarantees originate from the deterministic shield and fallback ladder, which the learned components cannot override.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

Although this safety framework is oriented toward heterogeneous manipulation tasks and uses explicit embodiment embeddings for conditioning, its design principles are relevant for cross-embodiment locomotion. Specifically, any morphology-agnostic body-motion latent actions that drive the B1 must be filtered through a safety shield that enforces limits on body velocity, foot clearance, and joint ranges, even if those limits are not explicitly encoded as a URDF.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) The JEPA world model can rank candidate body-motion commands based on predicted progress and risk inferred from egocentric video latents, but a separate safety module, possibly learned or hand-crafted for B1, should be responsible for rejecting commands that risk falls or collisions. This separation ensures that the cross-embodiment latent actions, which are inferred from insect egocentric video and may be miscalibrated, cannot directly cause unsafe behavior without passing through a safety filter.

## Latent Action Learning from Egocentric Video

### Latent Action Models and HiLAM: Extracting Skills from Actionless Data

Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged as a prominent approach to extract latent actions directly from observation-only data, enabling learning from large-scale actionless video for applications ranging from robotic control to interactive world models.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The basic idea of LAMs is to treat the dynamics of observations as being driven by latent actions that are not explicitly observed, and to learn an encoder that maps observation sequences into latent action tokens and a dynamics model that predicts future observations conditioned on these tokens.[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) These latent actions can then be used for pretraining vision-language-action models with actionless data, transferring actions across different embodiments, or enabling interaction within learned world models.[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

Existing LAMs, however, typically focus on short-horizon frame transitions, capturing low-level motion but overlooking longer-term temporal structure and high-level skills.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) HiLAM, the Hierarchical Latent Action Model, addresses this by discovering latent skills that model long-term temporal information, building on a pretrained LAM as a low-level extractor.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) HiLAM aggregates sequences of latent actions, which contain underlying dynamic patterns of the video, into higher-level latent skills that span extended temporal horizons.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Architecturally, HiLAM uses a hierarchy where the lower level extracts per-step latent actions from local frame transitions, and the higher level aggregates these into skill codes that describe coherent behaviors such as walking in a particular direction or executing a reach-and-grasp sequence.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Experiments demonstrate that HiLAM improves over baseline LAMs and exhibits robust dynamic skill discovery in complex videos.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

For our locomotion context, latent action models provide the core mechanism for extracting body-motion commands from egocentric video, even when no explicit action labels are available. The insect’s egocentric video contains rich patterns of motion: periodic oscillations corresponding to gait cycles, forward translations and yaw rotations arising from walking, and lateral motions when turning or sidestepping. A LAM trained on these videos could discover latent action tokens that correlate with these motions, and HiLAM could then aggregate them into high-level locomotion skills, such as “walk forward at Froude number Fr ≈ 0.2” or “turn right while moving forward.”[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The challenge is to ensure that these latent actions and skills are expressed in a morphology-agnostic coordinate system, which likely requires incorporating explicit body-frame transformation and Froude scaling into the latent decoding, as discussed later.

Moreover, once latent actions are discovered from insect egocentric video, a JEPA world model can be trained to predict future video embeddings conditioned on these latent actions, effectively learning a latent-action-conditioned world model analogous to LAC-WM but derived from actionless data.[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) This world model can then be used to imagine the consequences of latent action sequences and to align them with B1’s latent dynamics through cross-embodiment losses that encourage similar SE(2) body-frame motion patterns under corresponding latent actions. HiLAM’s hierarchical skills provide a natural bridge between insect and B1 locomotion: if high-level skill latents represent morphology-agnostic locomotion behaviors, we can aim to train B1 controllers that realize these skills in its own dynamics.

### UWM-JEPA and Latent Geometry for Counterfactual Imagination

Returning to UWM-JEPA, its emphasis on preserving the joint-state spectrum during rollout under unitary dynamics offers a theoretical tool for handling counterfactual imagination in partially observed locomotion environments, where egocentric video provides only a slice of the full state.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>) In the hidden-velocity indicator task studied in UWM-JEPA, the model must perform five-step forward simulation under a given action sequence with the target observation masked, and UWM-JEPA reaches 0.77 accuracy with performance degrading monotonically as actions are perturbed, while a parameter-matched LSTM-JEPA collapses to majority-class accuracy under every action condition.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>) Under blind rollout, UWM-JEPA loses fewer than ten points of probe \(R^2\) at short horizons, whereas vector-latent baselines lose forty-one and sixty-eight points, demonstrating more stable and informative latent dynamics.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>)

## Morphology-Agnostic Locomotion and Legged Robots

### Quadrupedal World Models via Morphology Conditioning

World models promise a paradigm shift in robotics, allowing an agent to learn the underlying physics of its environment once and then reuse that knowledge for planning and behavior learning.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) However, current world models are often **hardware-locked specialists**, meaning that a model trained on one robot, such as a Boston Dynamics Spot, fails catastrophically when transferred to another robot, such as a Unitree Go1, due to mismatches in kinematic and dynamic properties.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) These models tend to overfit to specific embodiment constraints rather than capturing universal locomotion dynamics, limiting their capacity for cross-embodiment generalization.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)

To address this, Danesh et al. propose a framework for training a generalizable Quadrupedal World Model (QWM) that explicitly disentangles environmental dynamics from robot morphology by conditioning the generative dynamics on the robot’s engineering specifications.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) Instead of treating static physical properties such as mass and limb length as latent variables inferred from motion history—an implicit system identification approach that creates adaptation lag and can compromise zero-shot safety and efficiency—QWM uses a physical morphology encoder that ingests the robot’s engineering specs as inputs.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) A reward normalizer further helps the world model function as a neural simulator that generalizes across quadrupedal morphologies, enabling zero-shot control across a range of embodiments within the quadrupedal family.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)

Empirically, QWM introduces for the first time a world model that achieves zero-shot generalization to new quadrupedal morphologies for locomotion, though the authors carefully note that the model operates as a distribution-bounded interpolator within the quadrupedal morphology family, not a universal physics engine.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) This work significantly advances morphology-conditioned world modeling but also highlights the importance of explicit morphology information for robust cross-embodiment transfer in legged locomotion.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)

From the perspective of the insect-to-B1 project, QWM presents both inspiration and a constraint. It suggests that disentangling environment and morphology in world models is achievable and beneficial, but it does so by **explicitly encoding morphology**, whereas our constraints prohibit access to URDFs or engineering specs for the target B1 robot.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) This means that any morphology-agnostic latent actions and world models must infer effective morphology information from egocentric video and possibly from proprioceptive signals, if available, rather than from explicit descriptors. Such implicit morphology inference may be feasible but is likely to be less reliable and slower to adapt than QWM’s explicit conditioning.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) Nonetheless, QWM’s architectural separation between environment dynamics and morphology-conditioned control can inform the design of the latent action and world-model components in our project, guiding us toward representations that treat SE(2) body-motion latents as morphology-agnostic and joint-level dynamics as morphology-specific.

### Planning in Learned Latent Action Spaces for Legged Locomotion

Hierarchical learning has proven successful for generalizable locomotion skills on walking robots, often leveraging a low-dimensional “latent” action space to communicate between a high-level planner and a low-level controller.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) Traditionally, this latent action space is user-designed, encoding desired motions such as target velocities or gait parameters, but recent work proposes a fully-learned hierarchical framework that jointly learns the low-level controller and the high-level latent action space, then plans over continuous latent actions using a learned high-level dynamics model.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) This framework generalizes to multiple robots, including Daisy hexapod simulations, A1 quadruped simulations, and Daisy hardware, and outperforms both learned hierarchical approaches from the literature and inverse-kinematics controllers under adverse conditions.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)

Technically, the framework consists of a low-level controller parameterization that maps latent actions into joint torques or position commands, and a high-level dynamics model that predicts how latent actions influence the robot’s motion over time.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) Once the latent space is learned, planning is performed in latent space using model-predictive control, optimizing sequences of latent actions to achieve locomotion tasks such as walking on rough terrain or following target velocities.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) The key is that the latent action space is **fully learned** and tuned to the specific locomotion dynamics of the robots, yet remains compact enough to support efficient planning.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)

This work is particularly relevant to the cross-embodiment locomotion project because it demonstrates that a learned latent action space can generalize across different legged robots without being hand-designed, and that planning over continuous latent actions can outperform both traditional controllers and other learned baselines.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) The insect and B1 robots, despite differing in DOF and morphology, could potentially share a common latent action space representing body-frame locomotion commands, learned from egocentric video and possibly proprioceptive signals. The insect’s low-level controller would map these latents to insect-specific gait modulations, while the B1’s controller would map them to quadruped-specific gait modulations, enabling cross-embodiment transfer via shared latent actions.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)

However, the cited framework still leverages explicit control signals and simulation data for each robot during training, whereas our project must operate without target demonstrations or kinematic models for B1.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) To reconcile this, we may need to treat the B1’s latent action space as being inferred from egocentric video alone, using latent action models and JEPA world models to learn a mapping from visual dynamics to latent actions, rather than from explicit joint commands. This will complicate training but is conceptually aligned with the latent-action planning paradigm demonstrated in legged locomotion.

### Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotion Policy

A complementary approach to latent action spaces focuses on training a transferable latent-to-latent locomotion policy that processes encoded latent observations to generate latent actions, enabling efficient and versatile motion control for diverse legged robots.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) In this framework, a latent policy is pretrained alongside diverse task-specific observation encoders and action decoders, with the policy operating entirely in latent space.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) The encoder maps raw observations into latent representations, the policy transforms these latents into latent actions, and the decoder maps latent actions into robot-specific control commands.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) A diffusion recovery module is introduced to minimize information reconstruction loss during pretraining, ensuring that essential information for decision-making and control is retained in the latent representations.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)

During fine-tuning, the pretrained latent-to-latent locomotion policy remains fixed, and only the lightweight task-specific encoder and decoder are optimized, allowing efficient adaptation to new robot entities and tasks.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) Experiments demonstrate that the pretrained latent policy effectively generalizes to new robot morphologies and tasks, leveraging prior experience across robots to accelerate adaptation and improve efficiency.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) This structure realizes the idea that a shared latent locomotion policy can capture general abstract motion skills that are independent of specific robot embodiment, while the encoders and decoders handle the embodiment-specific aspects.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)

For the insect-to-B1 scenario, this architecture offers an appealing blueprint: we would ideally learn a shared latent locomotion policy from insect egocentric video and possibly from other robots in simulation, then adapt a B1-specific decoder that maps latent actions into B1 control commands, without changing the latent policy.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) The challenge is that we lack B1 demonstration data or explicit encoders/decoders, and must rely on egocentric video alone to infer both the latent policy and the B1 decoder. Nonetheless, the latent-to-latent structure underscores the feasibility of separating morphology-agnostic locomotion skills from embodiment-specific control, which is precisely the conceptual goal of the proposed Froude-scaled body-motion coordinate.

### Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal Locomotion with Multi-Modal RL

Real-world quadrupedal locomotion over rough terrains, steep slopes, and high-rise stairs requires integrating proprioceptive and exteroceptive sensing, resilient control under out-of-distribution situations, and efficient reinforcement learning pipelines.[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) DreamWaQ++, an obstacle-aware quadrupedal locomotion controller for a Unitree Go1, addresses these challenges by fusing proprioception (joint states, IMU data) and exteroception (e.g., LiDAR or depth sensors) within a multi-modal RL framework that yields agile locomotion across diverse real-world courses.[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) The controller is sensor-agnostic, allowing integration with various options of exteroceptive sensors, and uses a single-stage RL procedure that remains efficient enough for real-time control and fast adaptation.[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>)

Although DreamWaQ++ does not explicitly focus on morphology-agnostic latent actions or cross-embodiment locomotion, it exemplifies the complexity of real-world quadrupedal control and the importance of robust perception and RL-based adaptation.[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) For the B1 robot in our project, a similar level of robustness may be necessary to realize the morphology-agnostic body-motion latents inferred from insect video, especially when operating on outdoor terrains or near obstacles. DreamWaQ++’s multi-modal design suggests that our B1 world model and control interface should integrate egocentric video with proprioceptive signals whenever possible, even if the latent action extraction is driven primarily by video, to ensure that the world model can infer contact states and body stability.

## Dynamics and Representation for Froude-Scaled Body-Motion Coordinate

### SE(2) Body-Frame Representation of Locomotion

To build a morphology-agnostic latent action interface between the insect and B1 robots, we must define a shared body-motion coordinate that is independent of joint structure and expresses locomotion in terms of body-frame motion. A natural choice is the planar rigid-body group SE(2), which describes translations in the forward and lateral directions and rotations (yaw) about the vertical axis.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) At each time step \(t\), we can represent the agent’s body-frame velocity as a vector
\[
\mathbf{v}_t = \begin{bmatrix}
v_{x,t} \\
v_{y,t} \\
\omega_{z,t}
\end{bmatrix},
\]
where \(v_{x,t}\) is forward velocity, \(v_{y,t}\) is lateral velocity, and \(\omega_{z,t}\) is yaw angular velocity.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) The SE(2) pose of the agent evolves according to
\[
\dot{x}_t = v_{x,t} \cos\theta_t - v_{y,t} \sin\theta_t, \quad
\dot{y}_t = v_{x,t} \sin\theta_t + v_{y,t} \cos\theta_t, \quad
\dot{\theta}_t = \omega_{z,t},
\]
where \((x_t, y_t, \theta_t)\) are global position and orientation.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)

In the egocentric video context, we can attempt to infer \(\mathbf{v}_t\) from optical flow and visual odometry derived from the camera images, treating the camera as rigidly attached to the body.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) The insect and B1 robots, despite having different morphologies, share the same SE(2) body-frame motion space at the level of their trunk or torso, and thus \(\mathbf{v}_t\) provides a natural morphology-agnostic representation of locomotion. Our latent action space should ideally encode desired changes in \(\mathbf{v}_t\) over short horizons, such as “accelerate forward” or “turn right,” which can then be realized by each robot’s own gait dynamics.

### Froude Scaling for Cross-Embodiment Locomotion

To make locomotion behaviors comparable across embodiments with different leg lengths and masses, biomechanics often uses the Froude number, defined as
\[
Fr = \frac{v^2}{g L},
\]
where \(v\) is forward speed, \(g\) is gravitational acceleration, and \(L\) is characteristic leg length.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) For our purposes, we can treat a normalized forward speed as
\[
\tilde{v}_{x,t} = \frac{v_{x,t}}{\sqrt{g L}},
\]
which yields a dimensionless measure of locomotion intensity that is invariant to absolute scale differences between robots with different leg lengths.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>) Similarly, we may define normalized lateral speed \(\tilde{v}_{y,t} = v_{y,t}/\sqrt{g L}\) and possibly normalized yaw rate \(\tilde{\omega}_{z,t} = \omega_{z,t} \sqrt{L/g}\) to ensure dimensionless scaling.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)

Although the cited works do not explicitly use Froude scaling in their latent action formulation, the idea of a normalized locomotion intensity is consistent with latent action spaces and hierarchical locomotion policies that must generalize across morphologies.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) In our morphology-agnostic body-motion coordinate, we can define the latent action at time \(t\) as \(\mathbf{u}_t = [\tilde{v}_{x,t}, \tilde{v}_{y,t}, \tilde{\omega}_{z,t}]^\top\), representing desired normalized body-frame velocities. The insect’s egocentric video world model can be trained to infer \(\mathbf{u}_t\) from observed visual dynamics, while the B1’s world model and controller can be trained to realize these normalized velocities with appropriate gait patterns and joint motions.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>)

The practical implementation of Froude scaling without explicit knowledge of leg length \(L\) and gravitational acceleration may require approximations. In simulation, we can estimate \(L\) from the distance between hip and foot joints in default stance for each robot, but our constraints prohibit explicit URDF access for B1. One possibility is to treat Froude scaling as an implicit normalization learned by the latent action model: the model can learn a scaling factor that equalizes the distribution of forward velocities across robots, effectively discovering a latent equivalent of \(L\) from visual dynamics.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) Alternatively, we can approximate \(L\) from camera height, assuming the camera is mounted at a consistent fraction of leg length across robots. In any case, the core idea is that normalized body-frame velocities should form the primary axes of the morphology-agnostic latent action space.

### Latent Dynamics Equations for World Models in Body-Motion Space

Given a body-motion coordinate \(\mathbf{u}_t\), we seek a world model that predicts future egocentric video embeddings conditioned on \(\mathbf{u}_t\), capturing the environment’s response to locomotion and the agent’s own motion dynamics. Let \(z_t\) denote the latent representation of the egocentric observation at time \(t\), produced by a JEPA encoder or V-JEPA 2-like backbone.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) The latent dynamics can be modeled as
\[
z_{t+1} = F_\omega(z_t, \mathbf{u}_t) + \epsilon_t,
\]
where \(F_\omega\) is a learned predictor and \(\epsilon_t\) accounts for stochasticity due to unobserved factors.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[\[2301.04104\] Mastering Diverse Domains...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.04104>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) A JEPA-style objective can be used to train \(F_\omega\) by minimizing the discrepancy between predicted and target latent embeddings:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{world}} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\ell\big(F_\omega(z_t, \mathbf{u}_t), z_{t+1}^{\text{target}}\big)\Big],
\]
where \(z_{t+1}^{\text{target}} = f_{\bar{\theta}}(y_{t+1})\) is the target encoder output for the next video frame or masked clip.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)

In the insect domain, \(\mathbf{u}_t\) must be inferred from egocentric video itself using a latent action model, producing a self-consistent latent-action-conditioned world model where both actions and states are latents derived from video.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) In the B1 domain, \(\mathbf{u}_t\) may be either predicted by a learned controller to achieve desired future latent goals (à la Demo-JEPA) or inferred from egocentric video of the B1 itself if some interaction data is available.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) The critical property is that \(F_\omega\) learns how body-frame motion influences the evolution of egocentric visual features and contact cues, forming the basis for planning and cross-embodiment alignment.

To capture hierarchical locomotion structure, a HiLAM-style model can be integrated by defining high-level skill latents \(s_k\) that summarize sequences of \(\mathbf{u}_t\) over longer horizons.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The dynamics at the skill level can be modeled as
\[
s_{k+1} = G_\eta(s_k),
\]
where each skill latent corresponds to a coherent locomotion behavior such as steady walking at a given Froude number or executing a turn.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Planning at the skill level can then be translated into sequences of \(\mathbf{u}_t\) via a learned decoder, providing a compact interface for cross-embodiment locomotion control.

## Proposed Architecture for Insect-to-B1 Cross-Embodiment Locomotion

### Source World Model and Latent Action Inference from Insect Egocentric Video

In the source domain, we have an 18-DOF stick-insect robot in CoppeliaSim with egocentric video, but under the project constraints we assume no access to its explicit joint commands or dynamics model for the purpose of latent action learning. The goal is to learn a world model and latent action extractor that represent insect locomotion in the morphology-agnostic body-motion coordinate \(\mathbf{u}_t\).[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

We can start with a V-JEPA 2-style video encoder and JEPA training objective, using masked prediction of video embeddings to learn a powerful motion-understanding representation of insect egocentric video.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[V-JEPA 2: Selbstüberwachte Videomodelle...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) Let \(f_\theta^{\text{ins}}\) denote the insect context encoder and \(f_{\bar{\theta}}^{\text{ins}}\) the target encoder, and \(g_\phi^{\text{ins}}\) the predictor. Training proceeds by sampling video clips from insect locomotion trajectories, applying temporal and spatial masks, and minimizing latent prediction loss between predicted and target embeddings, ensuring that \(f_\theta^{\text{ins}}\) learns motion-sensitive latents.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)

Next, a latent action model is trained on these insect latents to infer body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_t\). A simple approach uses a temporal dynamics model \(H_\psi\) that predicts latents conditioned on inferred actions, optimizing a variational objective that encourages \(\mathbf{u}_t\) to explain changes in \(z_t\) over time.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Concretely, we can define
\[
z_{t+1} \approx H_\psi(z_t, \mathbf{u}_t),
\]
and train \(\mathbf{u}_t\) as latent variables that maximize the likelihood of observed latent transitions. To enforce interpretability and morphology-agnostic semantics, we can regularize \(\mathbf{u}_t\) to align with visual odometry estimates of SE(2) motion, using self-supervised losses that encourage consistency between \(\mathbf{u}_t\) and estimated camera translation and rotation from optical flow.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) Over time, \(\mathbf{u}_t\) should converge to represent normalized forward, lateral, and yaw velocities in a Froude-scaled coordinate.

HiLAM can then be applied to discover high-level locomotion skills from sequences of \(\mathbf{u}_t\), aggregating short-horizon actions into skill latents \(s_k^{\text{ins}}\) that represent behaviors like “steady forward walking” or “turning right while moving.”[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The insect world model is thus a hierarchy: at the base level, \(z_t^{\text{ins}} = f_\theta^{\text{ins}}(y_t)\) encodes egocentric observations; latent actions \(\mathbf{u}_t\) explain short-horizon transitions; and skills \(s_k^{\text{ins}}\) summarize longer behaviors.

### Target B1 World Model and Control Interface from Egocentric Video

In the target domain, we have a Unitree B1 quadruped in MuJoCo, again with egocentric video but subject to constraints of no URDF, no kinematic model, and no demonstration data for locomotion tasks. The B1 must be able to realize body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_t\) inferred from insect video using its own gait dynamics.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>)

A similar V-JEPA 2-inspired encoder \(f_\theta^{\text{B1}}\) can be trained on B1 egocentric video, possibly in simulation, using masked prediction objectives to learn motion-sensitive latents \(z_t^{\text{B1}}\).[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[V-JEPA 2: Selbstüberwachte Videomodelle...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) If any interaction data is available for B1—e.g., RL trajectories from a locomotion controller like DreamWaQ++—we can augment the training with action-conditioned JEPA objectives, treating joint commands or motor torques as actions.[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) However, under the strict constraint of no target demonstrations, we may need to rely solely on unsupervised training of \(f_\theta^{\text{B1}}\) and latent action inference similar to the insect domain, extracting \(\mathbf{u}_t^{\text{B1}}\) from B1 video via a latent action model.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

To realize \(\mathbf{u}_t\) in terms of B1 joint dynamics, a latent-to-latent locomotion policy architecture can be adapted.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) An encoder \(e_\gamma\) maps B1 proprioceptive and visual inputs into a latent state \(h_t^{\text{B1}}\), and a decoder \(d_\delta\) maps latent actions \(\mathbf{u}_t\) and state latents \(h_t^{\text{B1}}\) into joint commands.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>) The latent policy \(\pi_\xi\) transforms state latents into latent actions in the body-motion coordinate, but in our scenario, the latent actions are externally specified by the insect-derived sequence, so \(\pi_\xi\) may serve primarily as a calibrator ensuring that B1’s realized motion matches the intended \(\mathbf{u}_t\).[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)

Training \(d_\delta\) without explicit demonstration data is challenging, but simulation-based RL can be employed: a reward function encourages the B1’s egocentric video and odometry to match desired body-motion commands, and the decoder \(d_\delta\) is optimized to minimize deviation between actual and desired \(\mathbf{u}_t\).[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) Over time, B1 learns how to execute normalized body-frame velocities, allowing the insect-derived \(\mathbf{u}_t\) sequences to be applied as target commands.

### Latent Trajectory Transfer and Planning

Once both insect and B1 world models and body-motion latent action interfaces are established, cross-embodiment locomotion becomes a problem of translating insect latent trajectories into B1 commands and planning their execution on B1’s world model. Conceptually, this mirrors Demo-JEPA’s approach of translating source visual demonstrations into target-compatible latent goals and planning toward them.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)

An insect locomotion demonstration consists of a sequence of egocentric video frames \(y_{0:T}^{\text{ins}}\), which are encoded into latents \(z_{0:T}^{\text{ins}} = f_\theta^{\text{ins}}(y_{0:T}^{\text{ins}})\).[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) The latent action model infers a sequence of body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_{0:T}^{\text{ins}}\) that explain the latent transitions, and HiLAM converts these into high-level skill latents \(s_{0:K}^{\text{ins}}\).[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) The goal is to drive B1 such that its body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_{0:T}^{\text{B1}}\) and resulting latents \(z_{0:T}^{\text{B1}}\) replicate the insect’s skill latents or body-motion trajectory, subject to B1’s morphology and safety constraints.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>)

A cross-embodiment alignment module \(A_\zeta\) can be trained to map insect skill latents into B1-compatible skill latents, using a JEPA-style predictive objective across both domains. For example, we can train \(A_\zeta\) and a joint predictor \(P_\rho\) such that
\[
P_\rho\big(s_k^{\text{ins}}, h_0^{\text{B1}}\big) \approx s_k^{\text{B1}},
\]
where \(s_k^{\text{B1}}\) is the skill latent that B1 should realize to match insect behavior, and \(h_0^{\text{B1}}\) encodes B1’s initial state.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) During execution, B1’s controller translates each skill latent into a sequence of body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\text{B1}}\) and joint actions via its decoder \(d_\delta\), while a JEPA world model predicts future B1 latents to ensure that imagined trajectories align with insect goals.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)

Planning over B1’s latent dynamics can use a CEM-like approach as in V-JEPA 2-AC, optimizing sequences of body-motion commands \(\mathbf{u}_{t:t+H}^{\text{B1}}\) to minimize the latent distance between predicted future latents \(z_{t+H}^{\text{B1}}\) and the insect-derived latent goals \(z_{t+H}^{\text{ins}}\) or \(s_k^{\text{ins}}\).[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) Safety shields filter out any candidate sequences that violate known constraints, and UWM-JEPA-inspired stochastic latent dynamics preserve uncertainty over unobserved terrain and contact states.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>)[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) In this way, the cross-embodiment locomotion pipeline becomes a composition of latent action extraction, latent world modeling, cross-domain latent translation, and safety-aware planning.

## Loss Functions and Training Strategies

### Predictive Latent Loss for World Models

At the core of both insect and B1 world models lies the predictive latent loss characteristic of JEPA-style architectures. For each domain, we define context observations \(x_t\) and target observations \(y_t\), apply masking, and encode them using context encoders \(f_\theta^{\cdot}\) and target encoders \(f_{\bar{\theta}}^{\cdot}\).[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) The predictor \(g_\phi^{\cdot}\) maps context latents into target positions, and the loss function takes the form
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{JEPA}}^{\cdot} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\ell\big(g_\phi^{\cdot}(f_\theta^{\cdot}(x_t)), f_{\bar{\theta}}^{\cdot}(y_t)\big)\Big],
\]
where \(\ell\) is typically an \(\ell_2\) loss or cosine similarity loss.[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) Masking policies are crucial: by masking future frames or regions, we force the predictor to infer latent targets from context, encouraging temporal and spatial prediction rather than trivial identity mapping.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)

For insect and B1, we can adopt the same loss structure but apply additional regularization that encourages the latent space to be sensitive to body-frame motion. For example, we can augment the loss with a motion-consistency term
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion}}^{\cdot} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\big\|M(z_{t+1}^{\cdot}) - \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{t}^{\cdot}\big\|_2^2\Big],
\]
where \(M\) is a linear probe that predicts estimated body-frame velocities \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_t^{\cdot}\) from the latents, and \(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_t^{\cdot}\) is computed via self-supervised visual odometry.[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) This encourages the latents to encode information about SE(2) motion, making them more action-relevant for locomotion control.

### Latent Action Discovery Losses

Latent action models such as HiLAM rely on objectives that disentangle action-like variables from latent dynamics. A common approach is to model latent transitions as being driven by latent actions and enforce that the dynamics model cannot explain changes in latents without them.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Formally, we can define a latent transition model
\[
z_{t+1} = H_\psi(z_t, \mathbf{u}_t),
\]
where \(\mathbf{u}_t\) is a latent action inferred from the data. The latent action discovery loss can include a reconstruction term
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{LAM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\big\|H_\psi(z_t, \mathbf{u}_t) - z_{t+1}\big\|_2^2\Big],
\]
plus regularization terms that prevent trivial solutions where \(\mathbf{u}_t\) is constant.

To make \(\mathbf{u}_t\) represent body-frame motion in a Froude-scaled coordinate, we can incorporate additional supervision from visual odometry by minimizing
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{SE(2)}} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\big\|\mathbf{u}_t - \tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t\big\|_2^2\Big],
\]
where \(\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t\) is the estimated normalized body-frame velocity derived from egocentric video.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>) This ties the latent actions directly to SE(2) motion semantics, ensuring morphology-agnostic interpretation. For HiLAM’s high-level skill latents \(s_k\), we can use clustering-based or contrastive losses that encourage temporally extended and coherent latent clusters, such as minimizing intra-skill variance of \(\mathbf{u}_t\) and maximizing inter-skill separation.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

### Cross-Embodiment Alignment Objectives

The core cross-embodiment objective is to align insect and B1 skill latents and body-motion latent trajectories such that similar locomotion behaviors map to similar latents across domains. A JEPA-style cross-domain predictive objective can be constructed using paired sequences of insect and B1 egocentric video that exhibit similar locomotion patterns, although under the constraints of no B1 demonstrations, such pairing may need to be synthetic or inferred from simulation.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)

Let \(s_k^{\text{ins}}\) and \(s_k^{\text{B1}}\) denote insect and B1 skill latents corresponding to similar body-motion patterns. We can train an alignment module \(A_\zeta\) with loss
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} = \mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\big\|A_\zeta(s_k^{\text{ins}}) - s_k^{\text{B1}}\big\|_2^2\Big],
\]
encouraging insect skills to map onto B1 skills.[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>) If direct skill pairing is impossible, we can use adversarial or contrastive domain adaptation losses that encourage the distributions of skill latents to be domain-invariant, such as minimizing Maximum Mean Discrepancy between insect and B1 skill distributions.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>)

At the action level, we can enforce that applying an insect-derived latent action \(\mathbf{u}_t^{\text{ins}}\) to B1’s world model yields similar predicted SE(2) motion as applying it to insect’s world model, by minimizing
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{motion-align}} = \mathbb{E}_{t}\Big[\big\|M^{\text{ins}}(F_\omega^{\text{ins}}(z_t^{\text{ins}}, \mathbf{u}_t^{\text{ins}})) - M^{\text{B1}}(F_\omega^{\text{B1}}(z_t^{\text{B1}}, \mathbf{u}_t^{\text{ins}}))\big\|_2^2\Big],
\]
where \(M^{\cdot}\) are motion probes.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>) This encourages the world models to respond similarly in SE(2) space to shared latent actions, despite morphological differences.

Safety and calibration losses from action-conditioned JEPA frameworks can be integrated by training risk and progress heads on B1 latent rollouts, ensuring that the cross-embodiment latent actions are ranked and filtered appropriately for safety.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) Calibration objectives, such as minimizing expected calibration error between predicted risk probabilities and observed collision frequencies, can further refine the latent risk heads.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

## Risks, Limitations, and Domain Gaps

### Sim-to-Sim Transfer Challenges between CoppeliaSim and MuJoCo

Transferring locomotion behavior between simulation environments such as CoppeliaSim and MuJoCo adds an additional layer of domain gap on top of morphology differences, as the physics engines, contact models, and rendering pipelines differ.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) While many legged locomotion studies demonstrate sim-to-real and sim-to-sim transfer using domain randomization and robust policies, these typically rely on explicit knowledge of robot morphology and control signals.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>) In our scenario, the insect robot is simulated in CoppeliaSim and the B1 in MuJoCo, and the only shared observation modality is egocentric video.

This raises concerns about whether egocentric video alone can provide sufficient information to align locomotion behaviors across simulations, given differences in lighting, camera models, terrain rendering, and body appearance. JEPA-style world models trained on internet-scale video have shown strong generalization across visual domains, suggesting that pretraining on large video corpora can mitigate some of these domain gaps.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[V-JEPA 2: Selbstüberwachte Videomodelle...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2506.09985>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>) However, the specific mapping between egocentric visual dynamics and SE(2) body-frame motion may still differ between CoppeliaSim and MuJoCo, potentially requiring domain-adaptive visual odometry and latent calibration.

One practical mitigation is to use unsupervised domain adaptation techniques in the video encoders, such as aligning insect and B1 latent distributions via adversarial training while preserving motion semantics.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Another is to incorporate simulation-specific augmentations and randomizations in training, encouraging the world models to be robust to visual domain differences. Nonetheless, the absence of explicit action and state correspondence across simulations makes this a significant challenge.

### Morphology and Dynamics Mismatch without Explicit Embodiment Information

Perhaps the most fundamental limitation of the proposed morphology-agnostic latent action framework is the absence of explicit embodiment information for B1. QWM’s success in zero-shot cross-morphology locomotion relies on conditioning the world model on precise engineering specs of each quadruped, allowing it to disentangle environmental dynamics from morphology and operate as a neural simulator across robots.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) In contrast, our constraints prohibit access to URDF or kinematic models, requiring that morphology information be inferred implicitly from egocentric video and, if available, proprioception.

Implicit morphology inference is possible to some extent: latent action models and world models can observe how the body responds to inferred actions and adjust their latent representations accordingly, learning embodied dynamics from motion history.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) However, this adaptation process introduces lag and may compromise zero-shot safety and efficiency, as QWM’s authors explicitly caution.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>) Without explicit constraints on leg length, mass distribution, and joint limits, the cross-embodiment latent actions may occasionally command motions that are feasible for the insect but unsafe or impossible for B1, requiring robust safety shielding and possibly conservative action filtering.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)[\[2409.19709\] Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal...](<https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.19709>)

Moreover, the assumption that locomotion behaviors can be fully captured by SE(2) body-frame motion ignores embodiment-specific constraints such as maximum acceleration, balance margins, and gait stability regions, which differ between insects and quadrupeds. A morphology-agnostic latent action space based solely on normalized body-frame velocities may therefore overgeneralize, failing to account for differences in what velocities are dynamically feasible for each robot. Incorporating implicit morphology latents into the world model, similar to LAC-WM’s embodiment embeddings or UWM-JEPA’s joint system-environment latent space, may be necessary to achieve robust cross-embodiment control.[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)

### Safety and Calibration under Cross-Embodiment Latent Actions

Finally, safety under cross-embodiment latent actions is a critical concern. Calibrated predictive safety frameworks show that action-conditioned JEPA world models can predict both task progress and physical risk for candidate action chunks and that coupling these predictions to deterministic safety shields yields improved collision-risk prediction and closed-loop success with low collision rates.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) However, these frameworks assume that candidate actions are defined in the native control space of each robot and that safety shields have access to detailed embodiment-specific constraints.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

In our scenario, candidate actions are body-motion latent commands derived from insect egocentric video and may not align perfectly with B1’s safe operating envelope. Safety shields can still enforce constraints on body-frame velocities and approximate joint limits, but the absence of explicit embodiment models may limit their precision. Moreover, the JEPA world model’s risk predictions may be miscalibrated when applied to latent actions outside the training distribution, such as extreme Froude-scaled velocities that were present in insect locomotion but not in B1’s training data.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

To mitigate these risks, conservative action filters and fallback strategies must be employed, as in the safety JEPA framework’s fallback ladder.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>) B1 controllers may need to clip or attenuate insect-derived latent actions to remain within safe ranges, sacrificing some fidelity of cross-embodiment imitation for safety. Calibration procedures, such as temperature scaling of risk probabilities and online calibration using observed collision frequencies, can help adjust the model’s risk predictions as more B1 experience is gathered.[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

## Conclusion: Toward Morphology-Agnostic Latent Action World Models for Locomotion

The current state of evidence in world models, JEPA architectures, latent action learning, and cross-embodiment robotics offers a rich toolkit for approaching the ambitious goal of transferring locomotion behavior from an 18-DOF insect robot in CoppeliaSim to a 12-DOF Unitree B1 quadruped in MuJoCo using only egocentric video and a morphology-agnostic latent action/world model. JEPA-based world models such as V-JEPA 2 demonstrate that predictive latent training on masked video can yield scalable motion-understanding representations that support planning when coupled with action-conditioned predictors, and latent action models such as HiLAM show that action-like structures and long-horizon skills can be discovered from actionless video.[\[2506.09985\] V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985>)[V-JEPA 2: Selbstüberwachte Videomodelle...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/de/abs/2506.09985>)[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) Cross-embodiment frameworks like LAC-WM and Demo-JEPA illustrate that shared latent action spaces and latent goal trajectories can unify behaviors across heterogeneous manipulators and robots, and morphology-conditioned world models like QWM illustrate the importance of disentangling environment dynamics from embodiment to achieve zero-shot cross-morphology locomotion.[Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal Wo...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08780>)[Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World...](<https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63978>)[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive A...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20811>)

At the same time, the proposed project’s constraints—no URDF, no kinematic model, no target demonstrations—push beyond the demonstrated capabilities of these systems. Current cross-embodiment world models and latent locomotion policies rely on explicit action signals, embodiment embeddings, or target-domain experience, whereas our setting demands that both actions and morphology be inferred implicitly from egocentric video. This increases reliance on predictive latent objectives, self-supervised visual odometry, and latent action discovery, and heightens the importance of safety shielding and calibration to prevent unsafe behavior under misaligned latent actions.[\[2603.05815\] Hierarchical Latent Action...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05815>)[UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25313>)[UWM-JEPA: Прогностические модели мира,...](<https://www.alphaxiv.org/ru/abs/2605.25313v1>)[World Models: Rendering, Simulation, Pl...](<https://aman.ai/primers/ai/world-models-jepa/>)[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)

Technically, a plausible path forward involves defining a morphology-agnostic body-motion coordinate in SE(2) using Froude-scaled forward, lateral, and yaw velocities, training JEPA world models for insect and B1 egocentric video that encode motion-sensitive latents, and learning latent action models that infer body-frame motion commands from video.[\[2008.11867\] Planning in Learned Latent...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11867>)[What Makes Video World Model Latents Ac...](<https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07687>)[Hierarchical Latent Action Model](<https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05815v1>) HiLAM can aggregate these latent actions into high-level locomotion skills, and cross-domain alignment losses can encourage insect and B1 skill latents to share semantics. Planning on B1 can use a latent-to-latent locomotion policy and JEPA world model to realize insect-derived skill latents, with safety shields enforcing morphology-specific constraints.[Transferable Latent-to-Latent Locomotio...](<https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17626>)[Demo-JEPA - log2r.github.io](<https://log2r.github.io/Demo-JEPA/>)[Meta AI Releases V-JEPA 2: Open-Source...](<https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/06/12/meta-ai-releases-v-jepa-2-open-source-self-supervised-world-models-for-understanding-prediction-and-planning/>)[Calibrated Predictive Safety for Hetero...](<https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2608.17496>)

Nevertheless, key risks remain, including sim-to-sim visual and physics domain gaps between CoppeliaSim and MuJoCo, implicit rather than explicit morphology conditioning, and calibration challenges in safety under cross-embodiment latent actions. Future experimental findings will need to validate whether egocentric video world models can reliably infer SE(2) body-frame motion and Froude-scaled velocities in both insect and B1 domains, whether latent action spaces learned in one embodiment translate robustly to another, and how safety shielding interacts with morphology-agnostic latent actions in practice. As the project progresses and new findings are logged, these theoretical and architectural insights can serve as a scaffold for interpreting results, refining loss functions, and adjusting model designs to move incrementally toward the goal of morphology-agnostic locomotion control from vision.

The egocentric-versus-allocentric material merged in above (Hu, Chen, and Lipson's egocentric self-model; Chen et al.'s allocentric full-body occupancy self-model) is not a side note to this synthesis but a second, independent confirmation of its central architectural bet: that egocentric vision is the viewpoint that ties the visual signal to the body-frame coordinate a locomotion controller actually needs, at the cost of the global context an allocentric view would give for free. This project's own measured result — a fixed third-person camera let the pose alone predict the command almost completely, and moving the camera onto the body was the first change to disturb that redundancy — is this same tradeoff observed empirically rather than argued from architecture. Hu et al.'s further result, that an egocentric self-model can detect and self-supervised-retrain through a changed body with no new sensors and no external diagnosis, is the closest existing precedent for treating a change of embodiment as something a world model survives by retraining rather than something that must be hand-modeled in advance.
