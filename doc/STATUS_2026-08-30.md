# Where this project stands, 2026-08-30, and what to do next

A self-contained brief for an outside reader. Everything numbered `F<n>` is an entry in
`doc/FINDINGS.md`; numbers measured today are marked **new**. Nothing here is quoted from memory --
every figure was read off the file or the run that produced it.

## The goal

Learn a **morphology-agnostic latent action** from video, so a behaviour recorded on one robot can
drive a **different** robot whose action space shares no dimension with it: an 18-DOF six-legged
stick insect and a 12-DOF Unitree B1 quadruped. No kinematic model, no retargeting. The only thing
the two robots share is what a camera sees.

The point of the pair is that proprioception cannot be shared at all between them, so vision is not
a convenience but the only channel. Published methods that cross robots either stay inside one
leg-count family or are handed a URDF and a retargeting stage.

## The pipeline

    frames ──► V-JEPA2 (frozen) ──► e_t
                                      │
    e_t, e_t+1 ──► inverse model ──► z_t          (training only: needs the future frame)
    a_t        ──► action projector ──► z_t       (control time: no future frame available)
                                      │
    e_t, z ──► forward model ──► ê_t+1

    planning:  for each of 12 recorded candidate behaviours, project its action, roll the forward
               model 5 steps, keep the candidate whose prediction lands nearest the goal embedding

Three adaptation stages on the new robot, following the source method (LAC-WM): fine-tune the
inverse and forward models (stage 1), fit the projector (stage 2), fine-tune projector and forward
model jointly (stage 3).

## What is established

| | |
|---|---|
| the frozen encoder carries morphology | a ridge regression on V-JEPA2 embeddings recovers an unseen body's segment scales, unsupervised (F20) |
| within the insect family, transfer works | held-out body, no retraining: 3.44 deg, R² +0.81, and swapping the latent between bodies moves the answer 0.04 deg (F49) |
| the quadruped is cheap to adapt, not zero-shot | frozen forward model on the B1 is worse than predicting no motion; one target clip clears break-even, ~7x fewer clips than from cold (F51, F52) |
| a joint-space target crosses only with a body term | -28.9 / -43.1 without, +0.61 / +0.57 with (F82, F83) |
| the closed loop works **within** the insect family | unseen body, world model frozen, 15/15 survival, 15/15 behaviour, 19.0% median speed error (F95) |
| **the adaptation objective matters, offline** | **new** three seeds per arm: contrastive 54.8% +/- 1.1 against MSE 21.6% +/- 0.3, chance 28%. MSE is *below* chance and its `/mean-z` is 0.985 -- it does not read the action at all, while predicting *better* (F119) |

That last row is the cleanest result of the session. MSE gets the lower training loss and the better
held-out prediction and cannot select; the contrastive term reverses both. **Prediction quality and
selection quality dissociate, and the objective decides which one you get.**

## What fails

**Cross-embodiment control.** Insect goal frames, B1 candidates, B1 driven, 36 episodes across
three loop settings and both objectives (**new**, F120-F122):

| | contrastive | MSE | chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| offline discrimination | 54.8% | 21.6% | 28% |
| loop, same-robot goals | 41% | 35% | 28% |
| loop, insect goals, commit 1 | 32% | 33% | 28% |
| loop, insect goals, commit 3 | 34% | 35% | 28% |
| loop, insect goals, commit 3, no warm start | 36% | 29% | 28% |

**The 33-point offline gap is 6 points on same-robot goals and zero across embodiments.** Survival
is 36 of 36 -- the robot never falls -- and the speed criterion is 0 of 6 in every cell.

**Turning does not cross.** Goal yaw against achieved yaw over six cells of six runs: r = -0.30,
+0.76, +0.33 (contrastive) and -0.18, -0.07, -0.66 (MSE). The sign flips within one arm between
settings. One run landed within 0.9% of its goal's turn rate, and the control inside the same batch
kills it: a **forward** goal produced +0.054 yaw where a turning goal produced +0.060. The robot
yaws 0.02-0.05 whatever it is shown.

Sideways fails everywhere, on every measurement this project has made.

**And forward does not track its goal either, which is the sharper way to put all of this.**
Achieved forward speed against goal forward speed: r = +0.13, +0.55, +0.54, -0.76, +0.60, -0.29
across the six cells, with the achieved range covering 16-39% of the goal range -- the quadruped
walks at 0.04-0.11 whatever it is shown, over goals spanning 0.017 to 0.222. **This reproduces
F111**, which measured corr(goal, achieved) = +0.074 over seven forward goals spanning 1.72x and
found the planner does not even select faster candidates for faster goals (-0.167).

So the honest sentence is **not** "forward crosses and turning does not". It is: **the loop
transfers the kind of motion weakly and the amount of it not at all.** Family selection for forward
goals is 52% against a 33% chance rate (down from F109's 71% on the old data), turning is 40%
against 33%, and sideways is 16% -- below chance. The robot is barely responding to the goal signal
in any channel, which is a different and more serious statement than one behaviour transferring and
another not.

## Two defects removed this session, neither of which was the blocker

1. **The two robots turned opposite ways** (F117). Every cross-embodiment turning result before the
   re-collection compared a left turn against a right turn. Now all three sets turn the same way,
   matched level by level (**new**, F118). The turning result did not improve.
2. **Three leaks in the adaptation chain** (**new**, F118): stage 1 drew its nine adaptation clips
   from the whole 48-clip directory, so two of the twelve candidate clips and three of the twelve
   validation clips were inside it; stage 2 fitted the projector on all 48; and stage 1's draw was
   not stratified, covering 6 of 12 behaviours. All three are closed and the results above are
   post-fix.

Also fixed: the closed-loop scorer graded cross-embodiment runs against the **demonstration** rather
than the **goal**, so every such run in this project's history was scored against a forward walk it
was never asked for. And stage 3 never wrote `action_dims` into its checkpoint, so the loop could
not load its own output -- these runs are the first time the full chain has executed end to end.

## Where the failure is, and what would discriminate

Three candidate causes. The evidence does not yet separate them, and this is the question we would
most like an outside opinion on.

**(a) The goal metric is not meaningful across embodiments.** The planner scores
`||FDM(e_t, proj(a)) - e_goal||²` as a raw MSE, and `e_goal` is a *hexapod* frame while the
prediction is a *B1* frame. Embodiment is strongly decodable from these embeddings (F43, F46), so
the distance is dominated by "which robot is in the picture" and the behaviour signal is a small
projection on top of it. **No centring is applied** -- the checkpoints carry no embedding offsets and
`center_embeddings` is false. This is the cheapest hypothesis to test and it has never been tested.

*Discriminating test, hours, no training*: run the planner offline over recorded frames with insect
goals (`plan_open_loop.py`). If selection is at chance there too, the loop's dynamics are innocent
and the metric is the problem. Then subtract a per-embodiment mean embedding from both sides and
re-score -- a change to the scoring function only.

**(b) The forward model's rollout does not survive five steps on the B1.** Long-horizon accuracy is
this project's weakest measurement. Offline discrimination gives the model the true next embedding;
the loop asks it to roll five steps into a future it has never seen from a state it drove itself
into. That is exactly where a 33-point advantage could vanish.

*Discriminating test, hours*: `does_rollout_matter.py` compares `rollout` against `direct` (no roll)
and `blind` (no action) on these checkpoints; `loop_frames_are_off_manifold.py` asks whether the
loop's frames are outside what the model was fitted on.

**(c) Pretraining is wrong.** The backbone is trained on the insect only, with a one-dimensional
shared body-motion target (forward speed). Yaw and lateral are supervised in no shared coordinate,
which would explain why forward crosses and nothing else does.

*Evidence for*: F83 -- adding yaw as a shared channel cost forward 68% and bought yaw at only
+0.37 +/- 0.27. The channels compete and the reason is not understood.
*Evidence against*: the model demonstrably can rank B1 actions (54.8% offline), so the
representation is not empty; and (a) and (b) are both cheaper to rule out first.

## The larger fork

**Candidate scoring may be the wrong architecture, and this was already decided once.** On
2026-08-28 the project chose **teacher-student** over candidate scoring (Q16 in
`doc/OPEN_QUESTION.md`) for a reason that today's results sharpen: scoring candidates requires a
target-robot policy that *already performs the behaviours* -- if no candidate turns the robot, no
scoring rule can produce a turn. Learning a policy requires only the ability to actuate, and data
where the robot falls is still data.

That would change the claim from "what fraction of steps picked the right candidate" to "does a
robot the world model never saw end up with a usable controller from video alone" -- which the
source method does not produce either. The cost is that distillation leans on long-horizon rollout
accuracy, i.e. exactly hypothesis (b) above, so it should be started on the insect, where the
forward model holds to half a second, not on the B1, where it does not.

**Our own reading**: test (a) and (b) first, because they are hours rather than weeks and because a
teacher-student pipeline inherits (b) unfixed. Do not touch pretraining until both are ruled out.

## What we would like an outside opinion on

1. Is the raw-MSE goal metric across embodiments the obvious flaw it now looks like, and is
   centring the right correction, or does it need a learned alignment?
2. Given a forward model that ranks actions at 54.8% offline and at chance in a five-step loop, is
   shortening the horizon a real fix or an admission that the world model is not a world model?
3. Is "forward travel crosses embodiments, turning does not" publishable as a bounded positive
   result with a mechanism, or is it a negative result that needs the teacher-student pivot before
   it is worth writing?
4. The contrastive-versus-MSE dissociation (better prediction, worse selection, three seeds) is our
   cleanest finding but is measured offline only. How much weight can it carry on its own?

## Limits of the evidence above

- The loop results are **one seed per arm**. F119's three seeds cover the offline claim only.
- Six goal clips per cell, so the yaw correlations are n = 6 and individually meaningless; they are
  reported as a set because the sign flips across them.
- On the B1, rerunning one configuration returns an identical number (F105), so spread has to come
  from different goals, not repeats.
- `S.R. behaviour` as printed by the scorer decides the class by the largest channel, and forward
  speed exceeds yaw even in the strongest turn condition (F108), so that column passes for free on
  turning runs and is not quoted anywhere above.
