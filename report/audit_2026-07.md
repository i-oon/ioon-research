### 2.2 The Phase-2 policy plan doesn't survive the repo
`direction_plan.md:108` assumes the long leg "may already have a usable trained policy." Every clause is optimistic:
- The mature checkpoints (`66k_aug3c`) that all scripts point at **are not in this copy** — only logs proving they once existed.
- The checkpoints that *do* exist are **34-dim obs**; **no `normalized_env*.py` in the repo produces 34 dims** (base = 36). The module that did was deleted. They likely won't load without reverse-engineering.

- Expert data = **one animal, one gait cycle, one trial** (`Animal06` → 67-row `ds_loopsm.csv`; the 30 `trails/` files are 30 noisy replays of that same cycle). **Invalid for a rescaled body** — same joint angles on shorter links ⇒ different foot positions, different torques.
- Cost: `NUM_STEPS=2e6` ≈ **1 day 4–10h per run** on the author's RTX 4060. You have a 2080 Ti.
- **No leg-length precedent exists** — every variant there is leg *removal* (18→15 dims) or terrain.

→ **Your IK-retargeting choice sidesteps all of this.** Correct call. AIRL is not on the critical path.

### 2.3 `z_t` is **64**, not 512 — a real misread propagating through your plan
`direction_plan.md:99` and `note.md:73` both state `z_t ∈ ℝ^512` "confirmed LAC-WM Table 4."
**Table 4's "Latent Dimension 512" is the IDM/FDM *hidden width*.** The paper separately says, §4.2:
> *"Both models employ an action embedding dimension of **64**."*

That is `z_t`. **Fix this everywhere.** Good news: 8× smaller latent, and it eases your compute problem.

## 3. What the papers actually say (corrections to your plan)

**✅ V-JEPA2 validates your frame-duplication trick.** V-JEPA2-AC §3.1: *"We use V-JEPA 2 encoder E(·) as an
**image encoder and encode each frame independently**… the encoder is kept **frozen**."* And pretraining itself
*"duplicate[s] an image temporally and treat[s] it as a 16-frame video."* Your approach has direct Meta
precedent. (Caveat: Appendix E.1 found naive repetition weak for image-VQA — different task, but worth knowing.)

**⚠️ V-JEPA2 saw zero simulated data.** VideoMix22M is entirely real video. The paper never studies
rendering-style domain gap — so your `PROGRESS.md §5` domain-clustering finding is **unexplained by, but not
contradicted by, the paper**. It also reports V-JEPA2 is motion-specialised (SSv2 75.3) but only competitive on
appearance (ImageNet 84.6 vs DINOv2 86.1).

**🔴 HiLAM is the wrong fallback.** It solves **temporal abstraction** (short-horizon → skill-level), *not*
embodiment invariance. Its chunking operator works on feature dissimilarity **between adjacent frames within
one video** — there is no cross-embodiment alignment objective anywhere in it. If `z_t` already clusters by
body, chunking **inherits and reifies** that clustering. Its experiments are 100% LIBERO tabletop manipulation.
→ **The right fallback is [UniSkill](Kim et al. 2025, CoRL) — "Imitating Human Videos via Cross-Embodiment
Skill Representations."** HiLAM literally uses UniSkill's IDM/FDM as frozen submodules. UniSkill targets the
failure mode you actually fear. **Swap the fallback.**

**⚠️ LAC-WM's fine-tuning is NOT few-shot.** It's the full 7,265-trajectory BFA set × 60k iterations
(20k/5k/35k across 3 stages). Your Step 2 (N = 5/10/20/50/100 episodes) is a **different, arguably better**
experiment — just don't claim you're following their protocol.

**⚠️ Compute reality.** LAC-WM = batch 512, 80k iters, **64 H200s × 4 days ≈ 256 H200-GPU-days**. You have one
11GB 2080 Ti. Mitigations: `z_t`=64 (not 512), small batch + gradient accumulation, fp16, shrink IDM/FDM depth,
and consider a smaller V-JEPA2 variant. Note the **tension you already flagged**: cross-augmentation forces the
encoder to stay in-loop, so you cannot pre-cache embeddings — that is your main memory constraint to design around.

**ℹ️ `s44182` identified**: *"Learning aggressive animal locomotion skills for quadrupedal robots solely from
monocular videos"*, npj Robotics 2025 (HKU). Explicit pose→3D→**hand-tuned biomechanical retargeting**→AMP.
No latent actions, no world model. **Related work / useful contrast**, not a component: it's the "manual
scaling law" alternative to your "learned invariance" pitch.

### The fix — reframe + one decisive experiment

- **Run the decisive ablation**: **latent-conditioned FDM vs. raw-joint-conditioned FDM (shared encoder)**.
  This *is* "EAC-WM ported honestly" to your setting. If the latent doesn't beat raw joints, the thesis is
  hollow — and you want to know that in month 1, not month 3. **This is not optional; it is the thesis.**

1. **Not needed for the task.** walk/turn/stop are large-scale whole-body behaviors, not fine-grained motions

4. **Cheaper check exists first.** The sim gives ground-truth joint angles for free — if occlusion is ever

**Revisit only if** Step 0/1 shows specific leg motions are systematically invisible due to occlusion — i.e.
a demonstrated failure, not a speculative upgrade. Then a second angle is a targeted fix.

## 6.5 NOVELTY AUDIT (added 2026-07-17, after reading the two suspected collisions in full)

### Verdict: the claim as originally written is dead; a narrower one is strong.

**Original claim** — *"First application of a latent action world model to locomotion for cross-morphology
transfer"* — **not defensible.** But both suspected collisions turned out to be **weaker than first assessed**;
each was downgraded after reading the primary source rather than trusting the survey pass.

| Paper | First pass | **After reading the actual PDF** |
|---|---|---|
| **Li et al., RA-L 2021** (arXiv:2008.11867) "Planning in Learned Latent Action Spaces for Generalizable Legged Locomotion" | "DIRECT COLLISION" | ❌ **NOT a collision.** Their "generalizes to multiple robots" means *the recipe is re-instantiated per robot*. Daisy and A1 each get their **own** latent space, policy, and dynamics model. **No experiment trains on one morphology and evaluates on another.** Daisy's `z` shares nothing with A1's. Even their broken-leg test **retrains the dynamics model from scratch**. They never vary limb length ("morpholog-" appears twice, both meaning "hexapod vs quadruped"). No vision in the model (camera = pose estimation only). No latent-structure analysis beyond one qualitative CoM sweep. Future work = gait transitions, sim2real, terrain — **never** cross-morphology, leg length, vision, or interpretability. |
| **Danesh et al. 2026** (arXiv:2604.08780) "QWM: Toward Hardware-Agnostic Quadrupedal World Models via Morphology Conditioning" | "DIRECT COLLISION" | ❌ **NOT a collision — complementary.** **QWM has no latent action model at all**: actions are raw 12-dim joint targets; no inverse dynamics, no action embedding. `h_t`/`z_t` encode *state*, not *action*. "Morphology-conditioned WM" ≠ "latent action WM". Requires **CAD/USD at test time, always** (PME is hand-engineered feature extraction Φ: USD → ℝ¹¹, not a learned encoder). Proprioception only (48-dim), **no pixels**. Cohort = 8 existing robots (ANYmal B/C/D, A1/Go1/Go2/B2, Spot) differing simultaneously in mass, limb length, stance, torque **and topology** — they state it is *"not merely a collection of scaled variants"*, i.e. **no controlled single-axis sweep, morphology axis confounded.** Their domain randomization varies **mass and CoM only — never limb length.** |

### The defensible claim
> **First to test whether a *vision-only, spec-free* latent **action** space organizes by **behavior** rather
> than **morphology**, under a *controlled single-axis* leg-length sweep.**

Do **not** contest QWM's "first world model for cross-morphology locomotion" — within their scope (explicit
CAD-conditioned) it is arguably true. Scope around it instead; the two claims don't touch.

### 🎁 What the "collisions" gave us

1. **QWM already ran our bet's ablation, and it works.** App. D, *"w/o PME (Implicit Identification)"* — remove
   μ, force the model to infer morphology from history: *"**successfully learns to maximize episode length** but
   plateaus at a noticeably lower asymptotic reward."* → **implicit morphology inference works.** And they
   **never tested it zero-shot on a held-out robot** — that is exactly our gap to fill.
2. **QWM hands us the measurement we need.** App. F-E: **silhouette score + between/within-class variance
   decomposition** on the latent (32,768 points). They measured only the *morphology-negative* side
   (`z_t` silhouette **0.033**, between-class variance **9.8%**). **We measure both sides** — by morphology
   (expect low) *and* by behavior (expect high). That two-sided result is the contribution.
3. **Our targets are exact mirrors** — QWM *engineers* morphology clustering into `h_t` deliberately via μ;
   we want the opposite in `z_t`. Complementary, not competing. Good framing.
4. **Li et al.'s baseline design**: 4 baselines including a hand-designed **IK "oracle"** they call *"a highly
   competitive baseline **rarely addressed** in hierarchical learning literature"*. Copy this — we need a
   classical hand-designed baseline, not just ours-vs-ours-without-X.
5. **Li et al.'s stress-test framing**: broken hind legs → IK collapses (0.91±0.06), learned degrades gracefully
   (0.62±0.06). **Our leg-length transfer is the same shape of experiment** — frame it the same way.
6. **We can beat Li et al. on rigor**: they report **no seed/trial counts** for their main comparison (Fig. 7),
   and their "sample efficiency" is a 3-point task-cost comparison, not a loss curve — and their "2000 samples"
   are *coarse transitions* (one per gait cycle), not episodes. Our planned loss-vs-episodes curve is **more
   rigorous than the prior art**. Say so.

### ⚠️ Three warnings

1. **🔴 URGENCY — QWM's stated future work is our direction.** *"Future work will extend QWM beyond 'blind'
   walking... We aim to **integrate visual observations**... and **disentangle task representations to support
   diverse skills**."* Paper is April 2026. Get the controlled sweep + two-sided clustering quantification done.
   (Mitigating: their vision is for *"geometry-aware planning"*, not to replace explicit μ.)
2. **They attack our premise directly.** *"implicit system identification... is **not only inefficient but

3. **Pitfall we will hit — reward/loss-scale heterogeneity across morphologies.** *"All configurations lacking

## 6.6 Larsen et al. 2023 — what the lab's own CPG actually does

Read in full (via the SDU open-access copy — Wiley/ResearchGate are bot-blocked).

- **Architecture**: 6 **fully decoupled** CPGs, one per leg — *"any connection or coupling was **not** defined
  between the neural control systems"*. Coordination is **100% emergent** through body↔environment interaction.
- CPG = 2-neuron recurrent oscillator, `w11=w22=1.4`, `w12=0.18+MI`, `w21=−0.18−MI`; **MI** sets frequency
  (stable range [0, 0.19]). `α` = plastic input weight, tuned online by a **dual-rate learner** from a forward
  model's foot-contact prediction error.
- Foot contact enters as `S1=−FC·cos(a1)`, `S2=−FC·sin(a2)` — **continuous negative feedback, not phase reset**.
- **RBF premotor net** per leg: 2 in (CPG outputs) → 40 Gaussian hidden (σ²=0.04) → 4 out (3 motor + 1 forward
  model). Trained **offline, supervised** (delta rule, η=0.1) on joint trajectories converted from the real

## 6.7 What the biomechanics literature says about our plan (all three agents converged)

1. **Foot trajectory is the WRONG invariant.** The stick-insect field (Cruse/Bässler/Büschges/Dürr) treats
   **AEP/PEP, duty factor, inter-leg phase** as the regulated quantities. Cruse's Rule 4 ("targeting") is
   *defined* on AEP. AEP/PEP shift systematically with load and speed = actively controlled set-points. The
   trajectory *shape between* AEP and PEP is comparatively **unregulated**. → Holding foot trajectory invariant
   fixes exactly what the animal treats as most flexible. **Corroborated independently**: Ajan YuChen's own
   notebooks never compute Cartesian foot trajectory — they compute duty factor, phase, tripod index, and
   joint-space cyclograms. `foot_traj` in that whole codebase is **only the Z coordinate**, used as a contact
   detector.
2. **Geometric scaling with unchanged timing is rejected.** Alexander: `L ∝ l` (our plan is right here) but
   **`f ∝ l^(−1/2)`** — a 0.5× leg should step **1.41× faster**; unchanged timing puts it at **half the Froude
   number**. STMR (T-RO 2025) independently adds a Temporal Motion Retargeting stage for exactly this reason.
   **BUT** Froude was derived for cursorial mammals with aerial phases; stick insects walk at duty factor ≈1
   (near-static) — the regime where Froude is weakest. **And real insects don't obey linear scaling either**:
   desert ants (*Cataglyphis*, JEB 2021) — 32% shorter legs but **40–50% stride difference** at matched speed.
3. **A yaw P-controller is not biological.** Straight walking is **emergent from local rules + bilateral
   symmetry**. Decisive evidence: *"Straight walking and turning on a slippery surface"* (JEB 2009) — **amputate
   5 legs and the remaining leg still generates the correct straight/turning pattern.** Real course correction is
   **event-triggered** (vision/antennae), and real turning = **asymmetric AEP/PEP shifts** (Dürr 2009), not a
   scalar gain.
4. **The nymph "natural experiment" doesn't exist** — Büscher et al. 2022: stick-insect leg:body proportions are
   **near-isometric across instars**. Nymphs are not short-legged adults. (My hypothesis was wrong.)
5. **The field's actual practice**: rescale the **controller**, not the trajectory. HECTOR scaled *Carausius*
   **20×** by rescaling Walknet, not a captured trace.
6. **The α=0.85 / β=1.2 "biomechanical scaling law" from s44182 is an orphan equation** — appears once (Eq. 23),
   never substituted into the actual optimization (Eq. 22), no derivation, no citation, no ablation, never
   validated across sizes of one morphology. **Do not cite it as support for anything.** (Its Eq. 22 is in fact
   just Peng et al. RSS 2020's retargeting objective.)
